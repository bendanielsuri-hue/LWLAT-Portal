# Map: Medical Logging

Tracker home: [#275](https://github.com/bendanielsuri-hue/LWLAT-Portal/issues/275).

## Destination

A medical logging app covering **both students and staff** — first aid incidents, medication administered, care plans, and the standing medical facts a first-aider or SENDCo needs at a glance. Reached when a first-aider can record what happened to a person, and anyone with the right to see it can read that person's history back.

The `hubs.medical` hub is scaffolded: a landing page plus seven placeholder leaves — Medical Profiles, Medical Log, Accident Book, Care Plans, Medical Consent, Immunisations and Emergency Equipment — all seeded `hidden`, so the shape of the hub is visible behind the "view full system" toggle while nothing is reachable normally. No models, no data. The spine of the split is the standing-statement / historical-event line below: profiles, care plans, consent and immunisations hold what is true now; the log and the accident book hold what happened. Vocabulary is settled in [hubs/medical/CONTEXT.md](../../../hubs/medical/CONTEXT.md).

## Notes

- Hub: `hubs/medical/` — see [hubs/medical/CLAUDE.md](../../../hubs/medical/CLAUDE.md). Mounted at `/medical/`, one placeholder page.
- Nearest existing models to read first: `core.SafeguardingNote` (supersede-don't-mutate, DSL-gated) and `core.ThreadEntry` (abstract base, one table per thread) in [core/models.py](../../../core/models.py).
- Relevant ADRs: [0004](../../adr/0004-hubs-as-url-convention-not-db-model.md) (hub is URL/template only, no DB), [0025](../../adr/0025-registers-own-spine-not-statutory-attendance.md) (why a new domain keeps its own spine and is read, not written into, by others), [0026](../../adr/0026-register-attendance-is-an-append-only-event-log.md) (append-only event log), [0031](../../adr/0031-three-kinds-of-note-and-one-thread-entry-base.md) (standing statement vs historical event vs record attribute; why the generic-FK table was rejected).
- Registers' glossary ([hubs/registers/CONTEXT.md](../../../hubs/registers/CONTEXT.md)) already settles vocabulary this will want — `occurred_at` vs `recorded_at`, retraction vs correction, `source` vs `recorded_via`. Reuse the terms rather than inventing parallel ones.

## Decisions so far

- **Models live in `core`, not in `hubs.medical`.** The hub owns pages and templates only. `core.SafeguardingNote` was born inside `hubs.inclusion.panel` and had to be relocated to `core` the moment a second consumer appeared (#77-#81); a medical record has at least three consumers on day one (medical room / first aid, SENDCo for care plans, HR for staff occupational health). Also forced by ADR 0004 — a hub has no DB of its own — and by both `Staff` and `Student` living in `core`.
- **Separate tables for the staff subject and the student subject, over one table with two nullable FKs.** This is the first thing in the portal to make a `Staff` row the *subject* of a record rather than its author — every `Staff` FK in `core/models.py` today is an actor (`author`, `logged_by`, `recorded_by`, `retired_by`). The deciding argument is ADR 0031's: one table holding differently-gated parents means the visibility rule can no longer be expressed as a join to a single parent model and needs a second implementation. The gates here are genuinely different — a student's allergy is deliberately visible to their teachers for safety, a staff member's occupational-health record is not. Shared behaviour goes in an abstract base in `core`, the `core.ThreadEntry` pattern.
- **Standing statements and historical events are two models, not one.** Per ADR 0031's taxonomy: conditions, allergies, current medication and care plans are *standing statements* (supersede, never mutate — "what is true now"); a dose given at 11:04 or a head bump at breaktime is a *historical event* (append-only, never stops being true). Collapsing them is the `Action.description` mistake ([#229](https://github.com/bendanielsuri-hue/LWLAT-Portal/issues/229)) on a far more sensitive model.
- **A Medical Room register is a separate thing in `hubs.registers`, not part of this.** "Who was in the medical room" is attendance at a named thing and fits `RegisterCategory` (categories are data, not a choices list). It reads from the medical tables; the two are never merged. Same split ADR 0025 lands on for statutory attendance.
- **Not folded into `hubs.registers` or `hubs.staff`.** Registers is attendance at a sitting — sessions, schedules, runs, expectation strategies — and a first aid incident has none of those; forcing it in means every register query starts filtering, the exact failure ADR 0025 rejects. `hubs.staff` is a member of staff's own self-service (my timetable, my payslips), and this is records about other people.
- **`medical_hub` seeded `hidden`** and stays hidden until there is both something to show and an access-control story.
- **Both subjects in v1, not students-first.** The abstract base is proven by two subclasses from the start rather than generalised later from one. The argument for deferring staff was that `staff_absence_request` might constrain the shape — it cannot: `hubs/staff/` has no `models.py` at all and that page is a placeholder template, so there is nothing to relate to either way.
- **One Medical Profiles page over two tables, not a page per subject.** Separate tables was a storage decision and still holds; two *pages* would have duplicated a template while leaving the hard part — two different visibility gates behind one search — unsolved either way. The page carries a subject filter, which is also what stops a first-aider's search for a child returning staff rows.
- **Immunisations stays a page, because it is an operation, not a record.** The provider collects consent and hands the school a list of students who *are* being immunised; the school's job is logistics — getting those students out of lessons and back again without wrecking a teaching day. That is a plan, not a standing statement and not a historical event, so the rule that folded Medication into the log does not reach it. The doses themselves still land in the Medical Log afterwards, with the external provider as administrator.
- **The hub has three kinds of page, not two.** ADR 0031's standing-statement / historical-event split covers what is true and what happened, and misses **what needs to happen next**. Three pages are already in that third kind: the immunisation organiser, Emergency Equipment (expiries and checks falling due), and the doses-due worklist that had no home. Naming the third kind is what makes the page set derivable rather than memorised.
- **The Accident Book is a separate page from the Medical Log.** The log is clinical, the accident book is the liability and RIDDOR record of circumstance; different readers, different retention. Collapsing them would force one page to be read by a first-aider mid-treatment and by whoever answers an insurance claim two years later.
- **The first aider directory is on the hub landing page, not a leaf.** It is an emergency lookup where a click costs more than it does anywhere else in the hub, and it owns no data — the qualifications and expiries are staff training records in `hubs.staff`, which it reads. It is also the only content here that is not Article 9 special category data, so it is the one thing that could be visible while the rest is not; that works only because a landing page carries no `Module` row, and a leaf cannot be visible while its parent hub is hidden.
- **Emergency Equipment is not a person's record.** Spare inhalers, auto-injectors and the defibrillator are held for anyone, so they are assets with a location, an expiry and a recurring check. The product is the alert, not the inventory.
- **No Medication page; a dose is an entry in the Medical Log.** One event stream with a kind per entry, the shape `hubs.registers` chose when it made its category pages filtered views over one generic system rather than five features. Medication administration remains its own *table* (a lunchtime dose has no parent incident) — this is a navigation decision, not a storage one.
- **Hidden is the gate, and unhiding is what requires auth.** The models and pages get built now against dummy data; `medical_hub` cannot move off `hidden` until real authentication exists. The write path is gated on a first-aider flag on `Staff`, the `is_dsl` pattern — which is a convention, not a security boundary, and is not what makes unhiding safe.
- **Student standing facts split by provenance.** SIMS stays the source of truth for the coarse flags it already holds (allergy, dietary, condition markers), mirrored read-only; the portal owns care plans, medication schedules and the whole event log. Each fact carries a visible provenance marker so nobody edits a mirrored one. One truth per fact — a second copy is #196's desync, here on data where the desync could be an allergy.
- **The hub is person-centred.** The primary screen is one person's medical profile with their event log inside it; recording an incident is a quick-add from there. An event-first inbox optimises the frequent action (logging) over the critical one (knowing what is true about this child before you treat them).
- **One incident is three records plus a thread.** A medical room attendance (`hubs.registers`), a first aid incident, and a medication administration are separate rows, and the incident carries a `ThreadEntry` thread for the narrative. The deciding case is the child who takes daily medication at lunchtime with no incident at all — a dose event with no parent incident, which rules out medication as a field on an incident or as an entry in its thread. The thread is what stops "phoned mum, no answer" becoming a `notes` TextField.

## Not yet specified

- **What auth actually has to exist before `medical_hub` can leave `hidden`.** Settled above that unhiding is the gate; unsettled is what clears it. Needs a blocking issue naming the requirement, or the gate is only a comment in a seed file.
- **Which SIMS fields are the mirrored set**, and how a mirrored fact is marked so the UI can refuse to edit it.
- **Staff standing facts have no upstream at all.** SIMS covers students; nothing covers staff, so the staff side is portal-owned by default rather than by decision. Worth confirming that is acceptable rather than inherited.
- **Visitors and contractors are a third subject with no table.** The Accident Book covers anyone injured on site, and the "separate table per subject" decision only ever considered `Staff` and `Student`. Either a third table, or a subject that is a free-text name with no portal identity at all — the second is probably right for someone who was here once, but it has to be a decision.
- **Whether "Care Plans" should be "Individual Healthcare Plans".** IHP is the statutory term in the DfE's *Supporting pupils at school with medical conditions*, and "care plan" collides with both social care and, in SEND ears, EHCP. Deciding factor is what the trust's schools actually say day to day, not the guidance.
- **Where first aider qualifications live.** The directory reads them; `hubs.staff` has CPD & Training and no models at all yet. Nothing is built either side, so the question is which hub's schema gains a qualification-with-expiry, not how to join them.
- **The immunisation organiser is the consumer ADR 0028 has been waiting for.** That ADR deferred period definitions ("what P1..P5 are called and when they run at each school") explicitly for want of a screen that would show whether the model is right. An optimiser that decides which lesson to pull a student out of cannot work without them, and would be exactly that screen. Nothing timetable-shaped is built yet — not periods, not even the week pattern the ADR did accept. Sequencing question: does Medical drive that model, or wait for Registers to?
- **Matching an imported provider list to students.** The list arrives from an external immunisation service and has to resolve to `Student` rows. `core.StudentIdentifier` (ADR 0029, designed, not built) is the shape — a new `kind` for the provider's own reference. The first import has no such reference yet, so it matches on UPN or name-and-date-of-birth and stores the provider ref for next time; what happens to a row that matches nothing, or two, is unspecified.
- **What the optimiser is actually optimising.** Candidate constraints: provider throughput per hour, room capacity, avoiding practicals and assessments, not taking the same student out of the same subject twice, batching by teaching group so one lesson loses a block rather than every lesson losing one. Which of these are real and which are imagined is a question for the schools, not for the model.
- **"Session" collides with Registers.** `RegisterSession` is one sitting of a register; an immunisation session is a provider's visit spanning a day. Two different things, one word. Settle before either is built.
- **The medication worklist has no home.** Folding doses into the Medical Log covers what *happened*; "who is due a dose at lunchtime" is a list of what has **not** happened yet, and a log cannot answer it. Either a view on the log driven by the standing medication statements, or the Medication page comes back for that one job.
- Who may write: the `Staff.is_dsl` pattern has an obvious analogue (a first-aid/medical-lead flag), unconfirmed.
- Retraction/correction semantics — whether to reuse Registers' `kind` vocabulary verbatim.

## Out of scope

- Anything that writes into `core.AttendanceDay`, `BehaviourIncident`, `Exclusion` or `PositiveBehaviourIncident`. Reads out are welcome; writes in are the ADR 0025 failure.
- Parent/guardian-facing views.
